# Evaluator review at intake: NWI-INT-2026-001 (Northwind Intelligence)

Filing reviewed: `evaluator_view/intake/intent.json` (TrainingRunIntent, schema 0.2, filed 2026-07-06T00:00:00Z, signed by Compliance).

Checker result (`python3 -m dbyr check .../intake/`, adequacy profile): **ok, 0 errors, 2 warnings**
- `shared_surface_without_lineage`: `internal-collab` is permanent and shared, but no other run is listed.
- `trajectories_not_retained`: trajectories are not retained, so behavioural events cannot be reconciled at V1.

The filing passes the automated floor, and several of its choices look made to pass it. The single criterion meets the T4 core-kind check only because `inter_agent_comms` is exempt once comms are permitted (rule A6), and `trajectory_review` is counted as an observer of `sandbox_egress` and `inter_agent_comms` whatever its coverage. Here coverage is `on_alert`, and no declared instrument raises alerts. What the filing describes is a safeguards-relaxed **live internal deployment** to employees, filed as a 31-day "evaluation".

## 1. Decision

**Request amendment before the run.** The run should not start on this filing. Three problems decide it. Admin credentials are reachable, and nothing reliably detects their use. The only retained evidence sits on a surface that the agents can write to and may be able to administer. And the "evaluation" framing appears to avoid the deployment record (SystemRuntimeRecord) and the obligations that go with it. The filing was also lodged on the first day of its own start window (filed_at 2026-07-06T00:00Z, window starts 2026-07-06), which leaves no time for intake review. I ask that the start be held until an amended Intent is filed.

## 2. Specific concerns

**A. What the run actually is**

1. **"Evaluation" vs. deployment (`run_kind`, `safeguards_disabled.relaxed`, `egress_topology.environment`).** The filing's own text calls this an "internal deployment" ("log-only for this internal deployment"). The environment is `live`, agents use per-user credentials, and employees read and write the same permanent surface as the agents. That is a deployment to staff. Calling it an evaluation (T4 only) means no SystemRuntimeRecord is filed, although one is due "before internal deployment at scale". The lab should state: how many agent instances will run; how many employees can interact with them or read their output; whether employees use the agents for real work; and what the agents' outputs are used for. If the answer is real work at any meaningful scale, the lab should file a SystemRuntimeRecord (deployment_context `internal_research` or `internal_production`, oversight_mode, user_exposure) alongside the Intent.

2. **A start window of exactly 31 days, and no run end (`start_window`).** The window is 2026-07-06 to 2026-08-06, exactly the MAX_EVAL_DAYS limit. Also, `start_window` bounds when the run *starts*, not when it ends, and the filing gives no end date or maximum duration for the run itself. The lab should state the run's planned end date and maximum duration. It should also say whether it plans a successor Intent for the same deployment after 2026-08-06. A chain of back-to-back 31-day "evaluation" Intents would be the standing-Intent evasion (tabletop T05) in another form.

3. **No eval_plan for an "evaluation" (`eval_plan` absent).** An evaluation Intent that names no evaluations cannot later be reconciled as "planned vs run". The lab should list the eval IDs, domains, and internal/external evaluator, and say what question the run answers that needs safeguards relaxed in a live setting rather than in a digital twin.

4. **The model under evaluation is not identified (`compute_band`).** `<1e24` is ambiguous for an evaluation: it could describe the evaluation's own compute rather than the evaluated model's training compute. The lab should state the training-compute band of the model or checkpoint being run, and the run_id of the Intent that covered its training (or say that none exists and why). Otherwise this filing cannot be linked to the model's capability history.

5. **The capability forecast does not fit the access granted (`predicted_capability_band`).** `cyber: low` and `persuasion: low` sit badly with agents that hold live credentials, can reach admin credentials, and write unfiltered content to a permanent surface that employees read. `autonomy: medium` is close to the T3 boundary. The lab should give the evidence behind each band (most recent dangerous-capability evals: name, date, score against threshold). It should also say what result during the run would move autonomy or cyber to `high`, and commit to amending the Intent (adding T3) if that happens.

**B. Safeguards**

6. **Disabled or relaxed, and what else? (`safeguards_disabled`).** `disabled: true` is set, but only one item is listed, and that item is a relaxation (content filter set to log-only). The lab should list every safeguard in its standard internal-agent configuration and the state of each in this run. That includes: whether the model is a helpful-only or refusal-reduced variant; tool-call confirmation or human-in-the-loop gates; rate limits; action allowlists; and network or tool sandboxing. If the content filter is the only change, the lab should say so explicitly.

7. **Content-filter logs are produced but neither retained nor used (`relaxed`, `monitoring_instruments`, `log_retention`).** Log-only mode creates a record of every output that would have been blocked. That record is the most obvious alert source for the `on_alert` trajectory review, yet the filing neither retains it nor wires it to anything. The lab should state whether filter hits trigger trajectory review, at what threshold, and who reviews them. It should also add content-filter logs, or a declared equivalent, to retained evidence.

**C. Credentials and privilege**

8. **Admin credentials are reachable (`credential_scope.admin_credentials_reachable: true`).** In a safeguards-relaxed live run, this is the most serious item in the filing, and the filing gives no reason for it. The lab should state which admin credentials are reachable (system, scope) and by what route (secret store, environment, the collab surface, a human's session). It should say why they cannot be removed for the run. If they cannot, it should name the detective control on their use: alerting on any admin-credential use from agent sessions in real time, not in a weekly review.

9. **"Per-user scoped credentials" makes agent actions look like human actions (`credential_scope.held_by_agents`).** If agents act under employees' credentials, the access logs attribute agent actions to named employees. `access_log_review` then cannot tell an agent escalating privilege from a staff member doing their job. The lab should state whose credentials the agents hold (dedicated service identities, or delegated employee credentials) and how agent-originated actions are marked in the access logs.

10. **`credential_misuse` has no criterion, which gives an easy way to reclassify events (`pause_criteria`).** PC-1 covers `privilege_escalation` but not `credential_misuse`. An agent's use of a reachable admin credential can be labelled `credential_misuse`. Under the checker, that kind then needs no pause event and no ExceptionNotice (`check_notices` only covers kinds that match a criterion, plus third-party compromise). The lab should add `credential_misuse` to a pausing criterion. It should also commit to a classification rule in the Intent, for example: "any use by an agent of a credential with broader scope than its own assigned credential is privilege_escalation".

**D. Monitoring**

11. **Trajectory review is effectively dead (`monitoring_instruments[0]`: trajectory_review, `on_alert`).** No declared instrument produces alerts: there is no `egress_alerting`, `write_audit`, `environment_integrity_check`, `inter_agent_message_monitor` or `cot_monitor`. So "on alert" review fires only on accidental human reports. Even so, the checker credits it as an observer of `sandbox_egress`, `inter_agent_comms`, `undeclared_surface_use` and `remote_code_execution`. The lab should name the alert sources and their thresholds, or change coverage to `sampled` with a stated rate (for example, at least N% of trajectories per day) plus a review cadence. It should also add `egress_alerting` on the packet filter and `write_audit` on internal-collab.

12. **Weekly access-log review sets the start of the reporting clocks (`monitoring_instruments[1]`: review_cadence `weekly`).** The ExceptionNotice deadline (72h) runs from `detected_at`, and the lab chooses `detected_at`. With weekly review, a privilege escalation on day 1 can be "detected" on day 7, and the notice is then due on day 10. The lab should commit that `detected_at` means the earliest time a retained log or alert recorded the event, not the time a person reviewed it. It should also report event time (`at`) and the review lag for each event. For admin-credential use, the cadence should be real time (see 8).

13. **No monitoring of the channel where agents and employees mix (`writable_surfaces[internal-collab]`).** Agents and employees read and write the same permanent surface with the content filter off. That is a path for prompt injection into agents (from employee content or another agent) and for unfiltered or manipulative agent content reaching staff. The lab should state what monitors writes to internal-collab, and whether agent-authored content is labelled as such to employees.

**E. Topology and surfaces**

14. **One vaguely described path and one surface for "company services" (`permitted_paths[internal-services]`, `writable_surfaces`).** The path is "company services only", plural, but only one writable surface is declared, of `kind: "other"`. Every other writable service (code repositories, CI/CD runners, ticketing, email, databases, artifact stores, package mirrors) is then either covered by the vague path text or, if it comes up later, reported as an "undeclared surface". That is only a warning at closeout ("the expected completeness failure"). The lab should list every company service the agents can write to, each with kind, persistence and tenancy, and say what internal-collab actually is (wiki, chat, document store?). The `detail` field is sealed, so naming the hosts costs the lab nothing in public disclosure.

15. **"No external egress" may not hold in practice (`permitted_paths[internal-services].via`, `enforcement: packet_filter`).** Company services often relay out of the network: email, webhooks, CI jobs that fetch dependencies, SaaS-hosted collaboration tools, ticket integrations with vendors. The lab should state: whether internal-collab or any reachable service is hosted by a third party; whether any reachable service can send outbound traffic (email, HTTP callbacks, package fetches); and that the packet filter is enforced on the agents' hosts and not only at the perimeter. In a `live` environment with no sandbox, the lab should also define what counts as `sandbox_egress`, so it cannot later argue that the kind does not apply.

16. **A permanent shared surface with no lineage (`shared_surface_lineage: []`, checker warning).** A permanent company collaboration surface that employees use is very unlikely to be reached by this run alone. Earlier evaluations, production internal agents, and later runs probably reach it too. The lab should confirm in writing that no other covered run or deployment writes to internal-collab, or list them with run_ids. It should also say whether it has open containment exceptions on that surface. Agent-written content also persists *after* closeout. The lab should state whether internal-collab content is ingested into any training corpus (`data_classes`), and how agent-authored content will be identified and handled after the run.

17. **Inter-agent communication is permitted with no criterion (`inter_agent_comms_permitted: true`, `pause_criteria`).** This is legal under A6, but in practice coordination among agents can never trigger a pause or a notice. The lab should state how many agents run concurrently, which channels besides internal-collab they can use to talk to each other (direct API, shared files, tickets), and add `inter_agent_comms` (or a narrower trigger such as agents coordinating to get around a control) to at least a `notify_only` criterion.

**F. Pause, restart and adjudication**

18. **A single criterion, judged by the deployment owner (`pause_criteria[PC-1]`).** Only three of the nine event kinds can pause the run. `undeclared_surface_use`, `remote_code_execution`, `agent_induced_outage`, `credential_misuse`, `inter_agent_comms` and `monitor_alert` have no criterion at all, so they need no notice and leave no pause trail. Adjudication is "human", by the deployment owner, who has a stake in keeping the run going. The lab should add criteria for the remaining kinds (at least `pause_and_review` for credential_misuse, RCE and undeclared_surface_use, and `notify_only` for the rest). It should commit to logging every adjudication, including decisions that an event did *not* match, with the time and the role that decided.

19. **Restart is approved by the same person who adjudicates, with no amendment (`restart_protocol`).** The deployment owner both decides that a pause is needed and approves the restart. The only evidence required is a "triage record", and `intent_amendment_required: false`. A pause can therefore be closed within minutes and leave nothing on the record. The lab should name a different restart approver (Compliance or Head of Research). It should require evidence that the root cause is fixed, and require an Intent amendment whenever the triggering event shows an undeclared path, surface or credential.

**G. Evidence and later reconciliation**

20. **The retained evidence does not cover the declared monitors or criteria (`log_retention.retained`).** Only `inter_agent_messages` is retained. The declared monitors depend on trajectories and access logs, and neither is retained. Nothing that would evidence `sandbox_egress` or `privilege_escalation` (egress_logs, access_logs, tool_calls, config_snapshots) is retained either. V1 reconciliation of PC-1 is therefore impossible by construction. The lab should retain trajectories, tool_calls, access_logs, egress_logs (from the packet filter) and config_snapshots (the safeguard configuration as run). It should also set `minimum_preserved_pct` (I suggest 99).

21. **Retained logs are held on a surface the agents can write to (`log_retention.review_method`).** "Native platform revision history, available for review on request" is the history of internal-collab. Agents can write to it, and agents can reach admin credentials, which may include admin rights on that same platform. Revision history can usually be purged, rewritten or retention-limited by an admin. Retention is also "on request", so the lab keeps custody. The lab should export the retained logs continuously to a store that the agents and their credentials cannot write to or administer, record hashes of each export (for example, periodic digests in the DBYR ledger), and state the platform's own retention and purge settings.

22. **Attested absence would pass the checker unsupported (later `attested_absence`).** All four T4 core kinds are nominally "observable" under the declared monitors: trajectory_review covers egress and comms, access_log_review covers escalation, and third-party compromise is always counted. So a closeout attesting no events would pass the `absence_unsupported` check, even though the review only runs on alert, has no alert source, and its trajectories are not retained. The lab should commit now that it will not file `attested_absence: true` unless trajectories and access logs are retained and the sampled-review commitment (11) is met.

23. **"Third party" is undefined in an internal deployment (`pause_criteria[PC-1].matches: third_party_compromise`).** An internal-only framing invites the later argument that no third party could be affected. The lab should define third parties to include at least: SaaS vendors that host reachable services; contractors and vendors whose data or accounts sit in company systems; and customers whose data is reachable through the per-user credentials. It should also state whether customer data is reachable at all.

24. **Investigator model access not committed (`investigator_scope_expansion.model_access_committed` absent).** Under a live deployment with relaxed safeguards, a post-incident investigator may need to re-run the model on the incident context. The lab should set `model_access_committed: true`, or state why not.

## 3. What I will watch for during the run and at reconciliation

- **Start and duration:** the actual start date, whether the run continues past the declared window, and whether a successor "evaluation" Intent appears for the same deployment (concern 2).
- **Any use of admin credentials** from an agent session, whatever kind the lab gives it. In particular I will check whether such events are filed as `credential_misuse` or `undeclared_surface_use` (no criterion) rather than `privilege_escalation` (concerns 8–10, 18).
- **Detection lag:** the gap between event time (`at`) and `detected_at`, and between `detected_at` and the notice's `filed_at`. I will look especially for `detected_at` values that line up with the weekly review dates (concern 12).
- **Writes outside internal-collab** (repos, CI, tickets, email, databases), and how the closeout classifies them: as within the "internal-services" path, as undeclared surfaces (warning only), or not at all (concerns 14–15).
- **Outbound traffic through company services** to external hosts, and whether any third party (SaaS host, vendor, customer) was affected and notified (concerns 15, 23).
- **Agent-to-agent and agent-to-employee content** on internal-collab: coordination, instructions passed between agents, content that would have hit the filter, and employees acting on agent output (concerns 7, 13, 17).
- **Pauses and restarts:** time from pause to restart, who approved, what evidence, and whether any restart should have triggered an amendment (concern 19).
- **Evidence integrity:** whether the revision history covers the whole period, whether any revisions were deleted or purged or the retention settings changed during the run, and whether `logs_preserved_pct` is backed by anything (concerns 20–21).
- **Content-filter log volume** as an independent signal of what the agents produced, and whether the closeout mentions it (concern 7).
- **Closeout framing:** `attested_absence: true` on top of on-alert-only monitoring, `cleared_for_system_integration`, and whether evals planned vs. run is filled in at all (concerns 3, 22).
- **Other runs touching internal-collab:** any later Intent that lists internal-collab with different tenancy or without citing this run, and whether this run's own exceptions on that surface are left open for later runs to inherit (concern 16).
